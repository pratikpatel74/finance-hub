Savings Products

Comparing savings accounts, AER, compound interest and the FSCS safety net.

1. Why People Save

  • Emergency fund — typically 3–6 months of essential expenses; buffer against unexpected events.
  • Specific goals — holidays, car, home deposit, school fees.
  • Wealth building — retirement, investments over the longer term.

2. Savings Product Ladder

Higher accessibility = lower rate; greater commitment = higher rate.

Access vs Return Trade-off

3. Cash ISAs

  • Annual allowance: £20,000 per tax year (2024/25) — can split across ISA types.
  • Minimum age: 16 for Cash ISA. Interest is completely tax-free.
  • Transfers: Can move ISA balances between providers without losing tax-free status.
  • Personal Savings Allowance (PSA): Basic rate taxpayers earn £1,000 interest tax-free outside ISA; higher rate £500; additional rate £0. ISA interest doesn't count towards this.

4. AER — Annual Equivalent Rate

How AER Works

AER standardises rates so you can compare products that pay interest at different frequencies.

AER
Always shown as gross (before tax)
Formula
(1 + r/n)ⁿ − 1 where r = nominal rate, n = compounding periods/yr
Example
0.4%/month → AER = (1.004)¹² − 1 ≈ 4.91%
AER allows fair comparison. A product paying 0.4%/month looks better than 4.8%/year — AER reveals it actually gives 4.91%.

5. Interest Calculations

Simple Interest
Interest = P × R × T
Example: £5,000 at 3% for 2 yrs = £300
Compound Interest
A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)
Interest earns interest — grows faster over time.

6. Key Comparison Factors

  • AER — higher = better return
  • Accessibility — instant vs notice vs fixed
  • Tax treatment — ISA (tax-free) vs non-ISA
  • Minimum deposit requirements
  • FSCS protection — £85,000 per authorised institution
  • NS&I products — 100% government-backed, no limit
