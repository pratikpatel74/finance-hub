Purposes of Money

Financial institutions, payment systems and the core functions of money.

1. The Four Functions of Money

💱
Medium of Exchange
Universally accepted for goods and services — eliminates barter.
📏
Unit of Account
Standard measure of value — prices can be compared and recorded.
🏦
Store of Value
Retains purchasing power over time — enables saving for future use.
📅
Standard of Deferred Payment
Allows debts and future obligations to be expressed and settled.

2. Types of Financial Institution

  • Retail (high street) banks — current accounts, savings, loans, mortgages, insurance. Examples: Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, NatWest.
  • Investment banks — wholesale services: underwriting, M&A, trading. Examples: Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan.
  • Building societies — mutually owned by members; focus on mortgages and savings. Largest: Nationwide.
  • Credit unions — member-owned cooperatives; common bond required; low-cost loans; not-for-profit.
  • NS&I — government-backed; Premium Bonds, Income Bonds, Direct ISAs. 100% government-guaranteed.
  • Post Office — acts as agent for Bank of Ireland (UK); savings, credit cards, insurance, travel money.
  • Insurance companies & pension providers — collect premiums/contributions; invest funds to pay claims or retirement income.
  • FinTechs & challenger banks — digital-first (e.g. Monzo, Revolut, Starling); lower costs, innovative features.

4. Personal vs Business Finance Needs

👤
Personal Needs
Transactional accounts · Saving · Borrowing (overdrafts, loans, mortgages) · Protection (insurance) · Investment
🏢
Business Needs
Working capital · Payroll (Bacs) · Business loans · Card terminals · Foreign exchange · Business insurance
Personal customers prioritise convenience and cost; businesses prioritise efficiency, cash flow tools and multi-user access.
