Providers of Financial Services

Banks, building societies, credit unions, NS&I, the Post Office and how they reach customers.

1. Provider Types Compared

🏦 Banks
Public limited companies (PLCs)

Profit-driven; owned by shareholders. Big Five: Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, NatWest, Santander UK. Widest product range. May offer lower savings rates than mutuals.

🏠 Building Societies
Mutual organisations — member-owned

One member, one vote. No shareholders → surplus reinvested for better rates. 75% assets must be mortgages; 50% funding from members. Major: Nationwide, Yorkshire, Coventry.

🤝 Credit Unions
Member-owned cooperatives

Common bond required (employer/community/religion). Max APR: 26.8%. Trade body: ABCUL. Not-for-profit; limited product range.

🏛️ NS&I
Government-backed savings

Executive agency of HM Treasury. 100% government-guaranteed (no £85k limit). Products: Premium Bonds, Direct Saver, Income Bonds. Distributed via Post Office and online.

📮 Post Office
Distribution channel

11,500+ branches UK-wide. Not itself a bank — agent for Bank of Ireland UK. Products covered by FSCS via Bank of Ireland UK. Vital for rural/underserved areas.

Building society demutualisation: Abbey National → Santander; Halifax → Lloyds; Woolwich → Barclays. Members received windfall shares at conversion.

4. Switching — Current Account Switch Service (CASS)

  • CASS — run by Bacs; guarantees a full switch within 7 working days. All direct debits, standing orders and incoming payments automatically redirected.
  • Inertia problem — switching rates remain low (~3%/year) despite CASS; customers perceive it as risky.
  • Revenue model — providers earn from the margin between APR charged on lending and AER paid on savings.
  • 2008 bailout — HBOS/Lloyds: government took 40% stake. RBS: 82% government ownership. Total cost ≈£37 billion.
