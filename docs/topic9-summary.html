Bank Accounts & Cards

Account types, overdrafts, payment methods, card types and the FSCS safety net.

Draft — update when college source material provided

1. Types of Bank Account

  • Current account — day-to-day transactions; debit card, cheque book, overdraft, standing orders, direct debits, internet/mobile banking.
  • Basic bank account — no overdraft, no cheque book, limited features. For those who don't qualify for a current account. Major banks required by law to offer these.
  • Student account — interest-free overdraft (typically £1,500–£3,000). For full-time higher education. Reverts to graduate account on leaving.
  • Savings/deposit account — earns interest on balance held; may limit withdrawals.
  • Packaged account — current account with added benefits (travel insurance, breakdown cover) for a monthly fee.
When overdrawn, the bank is the creditor and you are the debtor — an overdraft is a form of short-term borrowing.

2. Overdrafts

✅
Arranged (Authorised)
Pre-arranged up to agreed limit. Interest at a set AER. Cheaper option — apply in advance.
⚠️
Unarranged (Unauthorised)
Over balance without agreement. FCA 2020: single interest rate; no daily fees. More expensive.

5. Banking Channels

  • Internet banking — 24/7 via browser; transfers, bill payments, statements.
  • Mobile banking app — fastest growing; spending analysis, card freezing, biometric login.
  • Branch banking — in-person; complex queries; declining in number.
  • Telephone banking — 24-hour; useful for lost/stolen cards.
