The Personal Life Cycle

How financial needs change across life stages — savings, pensions, mortgages and protection.

1. Life Stages & Financial Needs

0–18
Childhood
Junior ISA (£9,000/yr allowance, managed by parents). No borrowing products. Financial education begins.
18–25
Young Adult
First current account · Student bank account (interest-free overdraft) · Student loans (repay above £25,000/yr threshold) · Lifetime ISA (18–39, 25% government bonus) · Building credit history.
25–40
Early Career
Regular income; saving grows · Cash/S&S ISA · Workplace pension (auto-enrolment) · First mortgage (5–20% deposit) · Protection: life insurance, income protection.
30–50
Family Stage
Higher spending on housing, childcare, education · Life insurance critical · Junior ISAs for children · Wills and lasting power of attorney.
50–65
Pre-Retirement
Maximise pension contributions · Mortgage may be repaid · Review life cover · Equity release may be considered.
65+
Retirement
State Pension: £221.20/week (2024/25, 35 NI years needed) · Annuity or income drawdown · Inheritance planning: IHT threshold £325,000.

4. Mortgage Types

  • Repayment — monthly payments cover capital + interest; fully repaid by end of term. Most common and lowest risk.
  • Interest-only — payments cover interest only; capital must be repaid at end (via investment). Higher risk.
  • Fixed rate — rate fixed for initial period (2, 5 years typical); predictable payments.
  • Variable / Tracker — rate can change; tracker follows Bank of England base rate.
  • LTV (Loan-to-Value) — loan ÷ property value. Lower LTV = lower risk for lender = better rate for you.

5. Protection Products

  • Life insurance (term) — lump sum on death within a fixed period; protects dependants.
  • Whole of life — pays whenever death occurs; more expensive.
  • Income protection — replaces ~50–60% of income if unable to work due to illness/injury.
  • Critical illness cover — lump sum on diagnosis of specified serious illness (cancer, heart attack, stroke).
  • Buildings & contents insurance — required by mortgage lenders; covers structure and possessions.
